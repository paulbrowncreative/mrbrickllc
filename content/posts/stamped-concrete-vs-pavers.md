Stamped concrete and pavers can both make a patio, walkway or driveway look like stone or brick. They're built very differently, though, and that changes how they behave through Michigan winters and what they cost to maintain.

## How each one is built

**Stamped concrete** is one continuous concrete slab. Color is added to the mix or broadcast on the surface, and while the concrete is still workable, textured mats press a pattern into it: slate, ashlar stone, cobblestone, brick or wood plank. Control joints are cut into the pattern, and the surface is sealed.

**Pavers** are individual units of concrete, clay brick or natural stone. They're set on a bedding layer over a thick compacted aggregate base, locked in with edge restraint, and the joints are filled with sand.

## Freeze-thaw and cracking

- **Stamped concrete** is a rigid slab. Like all concrete, it will crack. Control joints are there to make it crack in straight lines where you won't notice. Random cracks can happen if the base settles or the slab heaves.
- **Pavers** are flexible. Individual units can move slightly with frost without cracking, as long as the base is right.

## Repairs

- **Pavers** win here. A settled area, a stained unit, or a trench for a utility line can be fixed by lifting the pavers, correcting the base and relaying the same pavers. Read [why pavers sink and shift](/blog/sunken-pavers-causes-and-fixes).
- **Stamped concrete** repairs are harder to hide. Cracks can be filled, and the surface can be recolored and resealed, but new concrete never perfectly matches old.

## Maintenance

- **Stamped concrete** needs resealing every few years to protect the color and surface, more often in high-traffic or salted areas. See [sealing concrete in Michigan](/blog/sealing-concrete-driveway-michigan).
- **Pavers** need occasional sweeping, topping up joint sand, and optional sealing.

## Salt and deicers

Both surfaces do better with sand for traction than with heavy salt. Stamped concrete is more vulnerable, because salt and freeze-thaw can attack the sealed surface and the colored top layer. Avoid deicers containing ammonium nitrate or ammonium sulfate on any concrete.

## Cost

Stamped concrete usually costs less up front than pavers for the same area, because it's placed as one pour. Pavers cost more to install, but their repairability can make them the better long-term value, especially on driveways. Your quote depends on size, pattern and color choices, excavation and base depth, borders and steps, and access.

## Which should you choose?

- **Choose stamped concrete** for a seamless look, a lower installed cost, and if you're comfortable resealing on schedule.
- **Choose pavers** if repairability, flexibility through freeze-thaw, and the look of individual units matter most.
