Chimney damage is easy to miss because most of it happens where nobody looks — on the roof. By the time a stain shows up on a ceiling, water has usually been getting in for a while. Here's what to look for, from the ground and from inside.

## 1. Pieces of brick or mortar on the roof or ground

Mortar sand in the gutters, chips of brick on the shingles, or fragments in the flower bed below the chimney mean the masonry is actively breaking down. It's the clearest early warning you'll get.

## 2. A cracked or crumbling crown

The crown is the concrete cap at the very top of the chimney. It sheds rain away from the flue and covers the top of the brick. Once it cracks, water goes straight down into the masonry. From the ground, binoculars will often show cracks, missing chunks, or a crown with no overhang.

## 3. Mortar joints washed out near the top

The top few feet of a chimney take the most weather. If the joints there are recessed or missing while the lower chimney looks fine, the chimney is at the point where [repointing](/tuckpointing) will still save it.

## 4. Flaking or spalling brick

Brick faces popping off are a sign of water freezing inside the brick. A few spalled bricks can be replaced. Widespread spalling usually means the top of the chimney is due for a [rebuild](/chimney-rebuilding).

## 5. Rust stains or a missing cap

A chimney cap keeps rain, birds and animals out of the flue. Rust streaks down the brick point to a failing cap or damper; no cap at all means water is going straight down the flue.

## 6. Stains on ceilings or walls near the chimney

Water stains near a chimney are often blamed on the roof. Sometimes that's right — flashing is part of the roof — but cracked crowns and open mortar joints cause just as many leaks. It's worth having the masonry checked before re-roofing around it.

## 7. A lean or gap at the roofline

A chimney that leans, or has a visible gap where it meets the roof or house, needs professional attention promptly. This goes beyond maintenance.

## What to do next

Most chimney problems caught early are repairable: a new crown, a cap, and repointing of the top courses. Waiting a few more winters is how a repair becomes a rebuild.
