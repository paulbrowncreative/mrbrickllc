Every concrete driveway cracks eventually. Most cracks are harmless, and some are even planned. A few, though, tell you the slab is moving or the base has failed. Knowing which is which saves you from paying for a new driveway you don't need, or from patching one that's past saving.

## Cracks that are usually normal

**Hairline surface cracks.** Thin, shallow cracks, often in a random pattern, are common and usually cosmetic.

**Cracks at control joints.** Control joints are cut into the slab so it cracks there, in a straight line, instead of randomly. A crack in a joint means the joint is doing its job.

**Short, stable cracks** that aren't widening, with both sides level.

## Cracks that need attention

**Widening cracks.** A crack that's getting wider year over year means the slab is still moving.

**Offset cracks.** One side is higher than the other, making a lip. That's a trip hazard, and it means settlement or heaving.

**Settlement cracks with sunken sections.** Usually soil washed out or compressed under the slab. See [sunken concrete](/blog/sunken-concrete-slab-leveling).

**Heave cracks.** Sections pushed up by frost, tree roots or poor drainage.

**Map-pattern cracking and scaling.** A crazed surface or flaking top layer, often from salt and freeze-thaw.

**Crumbling edges and aprons**, where support at the slab edge has failed.

## Repair options, from least to most invasive

1. **Crack sealing.** Keeps water out of stable cracks so freeze-thaw doesn't widen them.
2. **Resurfacing.** A new bonded surface over a slab that's sound but scaled or worn. Read [resurface or replace](/blog/resurface-or-replace-concrete).
3. **Leveling.** Lifting sunken panels that are still intact.
4. **Panel replacement.** Cutting out and replacing only the failed panels between control joints. New concrete won't exactly match the old color.
5. **Full replacement.** When problems are spread across the driveway. See [what makes a driveway last](/blog/concrete-driveway-replacement-guide).

## A simple rule of thumb

If the problems are limited to **a few panels or the surface**, repair is usually worth it. If there's **widespread cracking, heaving and settling**, replacement is generally the better long-term value.

## Fix what caused it

Downspouts emptying onto the driveway, grading that sends water under the slab, heavy vehicles on the edges, and heavy salt use all shorten a driveway's life. A repair lasts longer when those are dealt with too.
