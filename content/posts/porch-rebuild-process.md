When a porch has settled, cracked through or pulled away from the house, surface repairs won't hold. The porch has to come down and be rebuilt on a foundation that won't move. If you're facing a [porch rebuild](/porch-rebuilds), here's what the job actually involves, so you know what to expect and what to ask.

## Is it really a rebuild?

Not every tired porch needs to come down. If the base is sound and the damage is to brick, caps or the surface, [restoration](/porch-restorations) is usually the better value. A rebuild is the right call when:

- The porch has **pulled away from the house** or tipped toward the yard
- The slab has **cracked through and dropped**
- Side walls are **bulging or heavily cracked**
- The steps have **separated from the porch**
- **Repairs keep failing** because the base keeps moving

Our guide to [why brick porches and steps fail](/blog/porch-and-step-problems) explains the usual causes.

## Step 1: Plan, measure and permit

Before anything is torn out, the new porch is laid out: landing size, step count and heights, railing locations, drainage, and finish materials. It's the best moment to fix what didn't work, like a landing that's too small or steps that are too steep. If your city requires a permit, it's pulled now.

## Step 2: Demolition and haul-away

The old porch, steps and caps are broken up and removed. Care is taken at the house wall, foundation and door threshold, and landscaping is protected where possible.

## Step 3: Excavation and footings below frost depth

This is the step that decides whether the new porch stays put. Footings are dug and poured below the frost line so frost can't lift the porch in winter. Many older Michigan porches were built without this, which is why they moved. Read [why frost depth matters](/blog/frost-depth-footings-michigan).

[[photo:footing-excavation|Excavating for a footing, the step that keeps a new porch from moving.]]

## Step 4: Foundation walls

Block or brick foundation walls go up on the footings to support the slab. They're usually block, finished later with brick, stone or a parge coat.

[[photo:porch-block-foundation|A new porch foundation being built in brick and block.]]

## Step 5: The slab and steps

A reinforced concrete slab is formed and poured over the foundation, sloped slightly away from the house so water runs off. Steps are formed with consistent rise and run from top to bottom, because one uneven step is what trips people.

## Step 6: Finishes

Brick or stone veneer on the walls, limestone or concrete caps, stone or paver treads, or a [paver](/paver-services) or stone deck over the slab. Anchor points for railings are built in so the railings end up solidly in sound masonry.

## Step 7: Cure, clean up, walk-through

Concrete needs time to cure before heavy use. You'll get guidance on when the steps can take foot traffic, and the site is cleaned up. New construction from Mr. Brick carries our 10-year warranty.

## Questions to ask any porch contractor

- How deep will the footings be, and will they be inspected?
- What's under the slab, and how is it reinforced?
- How will the new porch meet the house and door threshold?
- When will we lose use of the front door, and for how long?
- What does the warranty cover?
