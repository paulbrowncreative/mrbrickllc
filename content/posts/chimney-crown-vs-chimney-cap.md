People use "crown" and "cap" interchangeably, but they're two different parts that do two different jobs. Between them, they decide whether water gets into the top of your chimney, and water getting in at the top is behind most of the [chimney repairs](/chimney-repair) we do in Southeastern Michigan.

## The chimney crown: the roof of your chimney

The **crown** is the concrete slab across the top of the chimney, around the flue tiles. Its job is to cover the top of the brick and shed water off and away from the chimney.

A well-built crown:

- Covers the full top of the masonry
- Slopes away from the flue so water runs off
- Overhangs the brick with a drip edge, so water drips clear of the chimney face instead of running down it
- Leaves a small gap around the flue tile for expansion

Many older chimneys have a thin "mortar wash" instead of a real crown: a layer of mortar troweled over the top bricks. Mortar isn't designed for that exposure, so these crack quickly.

## The chimney cap: the lid over the flue

The **cap** is the metal cover that sits over the flue opening, usually with a screened side and a small roof on top. It keeps rain, snow, leaves, birds and animals out of the flue while letting smoke and gases escape.

Without a cap, rain falls straight down the flue. That water can rust dampers, soak the smoke chamber, and stain the firebox and the ceiling around the fireplace.

## How crowns and caps fail in Michigan

- **Freeze-thaw cracking.** Water gets into a small crack in the crown, freezes and widens it. Then more water gets in.
- **No overhang.** Water runs straight down the brick face and soaks the top courses.
- **Too flat.** Water pools instead of shedding.
- **Rusted or missing caps.** Cheap caps corrode. Storms and animals knock loose caps off.
- **Tight flue tile.** When a crown is bonded hard to the flue, heat movement cracks it.

## Signs your crown or cap needs attention

From the ground with binoculars, or in photos from a roofer:

- Cracks across the crown, or gaps where it meets the flue tiles
- Crumbling edges or pieces of concrete on the roof
- Loose, spalling or missing brick in the top few courses
- A missing, rusted or tilted cap
- Water stains near the chimney inside, or in the firebox

Our list of [chimney warning signs](/blog/chimney-repair-warning-signs) covers what to look for from inside the house too.

## Repair or replace?

- **Hairline cracks in a sound crown** can sometimes be sealed.
- **A broken, crumbling or flat crown**, or a mortar wash, should be replaced with a properly formed, sloped crown with an overhang.
- **A cap** is a straightforward installation or replacement.

If the brick under the crown has already loosened, replacing the crown alone won't hold. That's when a partial [chimney rebuild](/chimney-rebuilding) from the loose courses up is the lasting fix. Read [what to expect from a chimney rebuild](/blog/chimney-rebuild-what-to-expect).
