Your front walk is the first thing every guest uses and a big part of your home's curb appeal. The three common materials, **clay brick**, **concrete pavers** and **poured concrete**, can all last decades in Michigan when they're built on a proper base. They differ in looks, flexibility and how they're repaired.

## Clay brick walkways

[[photo:herringbone-brick-walk|A red brick walkway laid in a herringbone pattern.]]

**Pros:** A classic, traditional look that suits brick homes and older neighborhoods. The color goes all the way through the brick and won't fade. Patterns like herringbone, basketweave and running bond add character.

**Cons:** Fewer shape and color options than concrete pavers. It usually costs more than poured concrete.

## Concrete paver walkways

**Pros:** A huge range of shapes, colors and textures, from brick-look to large-format and stone-look. Like brick, pavers are individual units that can be lifted and relaid if an area settles.

**Cons:** Colors can slowly weather over time. Joints need occasional topping up with sand.

[[photo:curved-brick-path|A curved brick paver path with a dark border.]]

## Poured concrete walkways

**Pros:** Economical and durable, with a clean look. A broom finish gives traction. Stamped or colored finishes are options.

**Cons:** Concrete is rigid, so it will crack, ideally at the control joints. Repairs are harder to blend in, and new concrete won't exactly match old.

## What matters more than the material

- **Base.** Excavation and a compacted aggregate base are what keep any walkway flat. Read [why pavers sink and shift](/blog/sunken-pavers-causes-and-fixes).
- **Edge restraint** for brick and pavers, so the edges don't spread
- **Drainage.** A walk should shed water away from the house, never toward it.
- **Transitions** to steps, porches and driveways, planned so heights line up and nothing becomes a trip hazard

## Borders and patterns

A soldier-course border (bricks set end to end along the edge) frames a walk and helps hold it together. Contrasting borders around a lighter field, or herringbone inside a running-bond border, are common ways to make a walk look custom without much extra cost.

## Which should you choose?

- **Brick** for a traditional home, especially one with brick steps or porch
- **Pavers** for design flexibility and easy repairs
- **Concrete** for value and a clean, simple look
