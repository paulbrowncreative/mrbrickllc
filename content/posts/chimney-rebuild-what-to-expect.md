Most chimneys don't need rebuilding. A new crown, a cap and [repointing](/tuckpointing) solve a large share of chimney problems. But when the brick itself has lost its bond, repair becomes cosmetic, and a rebuild is the only fix that lasts.

## Signs a chimney needs rebuilding, not repair

- The chimney **leans** or is visibly out of plumb
- **Bricks near the top move** when pushed, or have already fallen
- **Large areas of spalled or crumbling brick**, not just a few faces
- The crown and top courses have **broken apart**
- **Repairs keep failing** in the same place

If the brick is solid and the problem is the mortar or the crown, a [chimney repair](/chimney-repair) is usually enough, and a good contractor will tell you so.

## Partial vs. full chimney rebuild

**Partial rebuild.** This is the most common. The chimney is taken down to the point where the masonry is solid, usually at or just below the roofline, and rebuilt from there. The part above the roof takes the worst weather, so that's where most failures are.

**Full rebuild.** Needed when deterioration runs the full height of the chimney, when the foundation or base has failed, or when the chimney is being substantially changed.

**Removal.** For chimneys that are no longer used, taking the stack down below the roofline and closing the roof can be an option. It's worth discussing with your roofer as well.

## How a chimney rebuild works, step by step

1. **Protection and access.** Scaffolding or roof staging is set up, and the roof and landscaping are protected.
2. **Dismantling.** The chimney is taken down course by course to sound masonry, and debris is lowered safely.
3. **Flue check.** Flue tiles or liners are inspected and replaced where needed.
4. **Rebuilding.** New brick is laid in compatible mortar, matched to the house or chosen new.
5. **New crown.** A properly formed, sloped concrete crown with an overhang and drip edge. This is the part that decides how long the new masonry stays dry.
6. **New cap** over the flue or flues.
7. **Clean-up.** Debris is hauled away and the roof and yard are cleaned.

Flashing, where the chimney meets the roof, is part of the roof. Coordinate with your roofer if it needs replacing at the same time.

## Protecting your new chimney

- Keep the **cap** in place and check it after big storms
- Watch the **crown** for cracks every few years
- Keep **gutters clear** so water doesn't back up at the chimney
- Have the **flue inspected and swept** as recommended if you burn wood
