Michigan concrete has a hard life. Water soaks in, freezes, and expands dozens of times a winter, and road salt tracked in by cars makes that cycle more damaging. Sealing is one of the simplest ways to protect a driveway, walk or patio, as long as you use the right type of sealer at the right time.

## How salt and freeze-thaw damage concrete

Concrete is porous. When water saturates the surface and freezes, it creates pressure that breaks off the top layer. That's **scaling**, the flaking surface you see on many Michigan driveways, especially near the street and where cars park. Salt keeps the surface wetter and increases the number of freeze-thaw cycles, and some deicers chemically attack the concrete itself.

Sealing reduces how much water and salt gets into the surface in the first place.

## Two kinds of sealer

**Penetrating sealers** (silane and siloxane types) soak into the concrete and make it water-repellent without leaving a film. They don't change the look much and don't make the surface slippery. They're usually the right choice for **broom-finished driveways, walks and steps**.

**Film-forming sealers** (acrylic and similar) leave a coating on top that enhances color and gives a satin or glossy look. They're used on **stamped and colored concrete** to protect the finish, and they need renewing more often. See [stamped concrete vs. pavers](/blog/stamped-concrete-vs-pavers).

**Garage floor coatings** are a separate category, designed for interior slabs and hot-tire contact.

## When to seal

- **New concrete:** after it has cured, usually about a month depending on the product and weather. Read [new concrete care](/blog/new-concrete-curing-and-first-winter).
- **Existing concrete:** in dry weather, on a clean, dry surface, with temperatures in the range the product requires. Late summer and early fall are ideal in Michigan, ahead of salt season.
- **Resealing:** every few years, sooner for stamped concrete, heavy traffic or heavily salted areas.

## Prep matters more than the product

1. Clean off dirt, oil, rust and old failing sealer
2. Repair cracks and spalls first
3. Let the concrete dry fully
4. Apply at the rate the manufacturer specifies. More is not better, especially with film-forming sealers.

## Sealing won't fix everything

A sealer protects sound concrete. It won't fix a slab that's cracked through, sunken or heaved. Those need [resurfacing, leveling or replacement](/blog/resurface-or-replace-concrete). Scaling that's already happened can often be [resurfaced](/concrete-resurfacing), then sealed.

## Winter habits that help

- **Shovel promptly** so snow doesn't melt and refreeze on the surface
- **Use sand** for traction and minimal deicer
- **Rinse off salt** tracked onto the driveway during winter thaws
- **Avoid ammonium-based deicers** entirely
