When brick starts to look tired, homeowners usually hear one of three recommendations: tuckpoint it, replace some brick, or rebuild the section. They're very different jobs at very different prices, and the right answer depends on one question — **is the problem the mortar, the brick, or the wall itself?**

## Start with the mortar

Mortar is supposed to wear out before brick does. It's the softer, sacrificial part of the wall. When joints are sandy, recessed behind the brick face, or cracked along their length, but the bricks are still tight and the wall is straight, the wall needs [tuckpointing (repointing)](/tuckpointing).

A simple check: press a key or flat screwdriver into a mortar joint. If it scrapes out easily and leaves a groove, the mortar has lost its strength.

Repointing replaces that mortar before water starts working on the brick. It's the least invasive repair and, done at the right time, it prevents the more expensive ones below.

## Then look at the brick

If the joints are open *and* individual bricks are flaking, cracked through, or loose, the damage has moved into the brick itself. Water that got in through the joints has frozen inside the brick and pushed its face off — called spalling.

The fix is [brick replacement](/brick-repair): cutting out damaged units and setting matched brick in their place, usually alongside repointing of the surrounding area. A few bad bricks in a sound wall is a routine repair.

## Then look at the wall

Rebuilding is warranted when the wall has lost its structure, not just its surface. Signs include:

- A section that bulges, leans, or is visibly out of plumb
- Bricks you can move by hand, especially at the top of a chimney or a freestanding pillar
- Stair-step cracks that run through both mortar and brick
- Repairs that have been redone several times and keep failing

At that point the bricks are no longer bonded to each other. New mortar in the joints would just be holding loose units in place for a winter or two. The durable fix is to take the section down to sound masonry and relay it. On chimneys this is usually a [partial chimney rebuild](/chimney-rebuilding) from the roofline up.

## Chimneys fail first

Chimneys get weather on every side, so they show all three stages sooner than the walls of the house. A cracked crown lets water into the top courses; the mortar there washes out first; then the brick loosens. Catching a chimney at the repointing stage costs far less than rebuilding it.

## Why the mortar mix matters

On older homes — especially pre-war brick — the original mortar was softer than today's standard mixes. Repointing with mortar that's harder than the brick forces the brick to take the stress, and the brick edges crack instead of the joint. A good mason matches the mortar to the wall, not the other way around.

## Fix the water, not just the masonry

Almost every brick problem is a water problem. Downspouts that dump against a wall, a failed chimney crown, missing caps on pillars, or soil sloped toward the house will undo any repair. Ask whoever inspects your masonry what's causing the damage, not just what's damaged.
