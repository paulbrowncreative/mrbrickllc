A masonry fireplace is really two things at once. There's the firebox, a heat-rated chamber that has to contain fire safely, and there's the finished surround and hearth that everyone sees. Both wear out, but in different ways, and firebox problems are the ones that matter most for safety.

## Know the parts of your fireplace

- **Firebox.** The chamber where the fire burns, lined with firebrick laid in heat-resistant refractory mortar.
- **Damper.** The metal door at the top of the firebox that closes off the flue when you're not burning.
- **Smoke chamber and flue.** The passage that carries smoke up through the [chimney](/chimney-services).
- **Hearth.** The non-combustible floor in front of the firebox.
- **Surround.** The brick, stone or tile face around the opening.

## Firebox problems and what they mean

**Hairline cracks in firebrick** are common. Firebrick expands and contracts with every fire. On their own, fine cracks aren't an emergency, but keep an eye on them.

**Open or missing joints** are more serious. The mortar between firebricks protects the house framing from heat. Gaps should be repointed with refractory mortar, not ordinary mortar.

**Loose, crumbling or missing firebrick** calls for rebuilding that section of the firebox, or the whole firebox, before you burn another fire.

**Water staining or rust in the firebox** usually means water is coming down the chimney. Check the cap and the crown. See [leaking chimney causes](/blog/leaking-chimney-causes).

## Hearth and surround issues

- **Loose or cracked hearth stones** are a trip hazard and can let embers reach combustible materials underneath. They should be reset or replaced.
- **Stained, cracked or dated surrounds** are mostly cosmetic. Refacing with new brick or stone is one of the most transformative projects you can do in a living room.

## Damper problems

A damper that's stuck, rusted or won't seal lets heat escape and cold air and water in. Dampers can be repaired or replaced, and a chimney-top damper is another option on some chimneys.

## Before any fireplace repair: check the chimney

A fireplace is only as safe as the flue it vents into. Before repairing or rebuilding a firebox, the chimney should be checked for a damaged flue, loose brick or a failing crown. Chimney problems often show up as fireplace problems first, like smoke coming into the room, water in the firebox, or debris falling onto the grate.
