A masonry outdoor kitchen, whether it's a simple grill island or a full counter with a sink and bar seating, turns a patio into the place everyone ends up. Masonry is heavy and permanent, though, so decisions made before construction are much easier than changes after. Here's what to plan.

## Pick your appliances first

The single most important step: **choose your grill and any other built-in appliances before the masonry is designed.** Openings are built to exact cut-out dimensions. Changing grills later can mean cutting into finished masonry.

Common built-ins:

- Built-in grill head
- Side burner
- Access doors and drawers
- Outdoor-rated refrigerator
- Sink

## Layout

- **Straight run.** Simple and space-efficient.
- **L-shape.** Separates cooking from serving.
- **U-shape or island with bar seating.** Great for entertaining, and needs the most room.

Plan **landing space** on both sides of the grill, keep the cook facing guests, and think about prevailing wind so smoke doesn't blow across the seating area.

## Structure: footings and support

A masonry kitchen is very heavy. It needs a **footing below frost depth or a properly reinforced slab** so it doesn't shift or crack as the ground freezes and thaws. Read [why frost depth matters](/blog/frost-depth-footings-michigan). Built on an existing patio, the patio has to be checked to see whether it can carry the load.

The body is usually **concrete block**, faced with **brick or stone veneer** to match the house or patio.

## Utilities

Gas lines, electrical outlets, lighting and water should be planned before the base and masonry go in, with conduit and sleeves placed ahead of time. Gas and electrical connections should be made by licensed trades. We build the masonry and coordinate with them.

## Countertops and caps

The counter takes sun, rain, snow, grease and freeze-thaw. Choose a surface rated for outdoor use in a freeze-thaw climate, with overhangs that shed water away from the masonry below.

## Pair it with fire

An [outdoor fireplace](/fireplace-services) or fire pit near the kitchen extends the patio season into fall. Plan the two together so seating, clearances and footings work as one design. See [fireplace repair and installation](/blog/fireplace-firebox-repair).

## Winter care in Michigan

- Cover or winterize appliances per the manufacturer's instructions
- Shut off and drain water lines before freezing weather
- Keep snow from piling against the masonry for long periods
- Check caps and joints each spring
