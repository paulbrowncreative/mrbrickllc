A new driveway, walk or patio is one of the best-looking upgrades you can make to a house. The first weeks and the first winter are when it's most vulnerable, though. Here's how curing works and how to protect your investment.

## Curing vs. drying

Concrete doesn't harden by drying out. It hardens through a chemical reaction between cement and water, called **hydration**, which continues for weeks. Concrete that loses its moisture too fast, from hot sun, wind or low humidity, doesn't reach its full strength at the surface. That's why installers keep new concrete damp or apply a curing compound.

## General timelines

These are typical guidelines. Temperature, the mix and slab thickness all change them, so always follow your installer's guidance.

- **Foot traffic:** usually after a day or two
- **Passenger cars:** typically about a week
- **Heavy vehicles** (trucks, trailers, dumpsters, RVs): longer, so ask first
- **Near-full strength:** concrete keeps gaining strength for about a month
- **Sealing:** usually after about a month of curing. See [sealing concrete in Michigan](/blog/sealing-concrete-driveway-michigan).

## Why the first winter is the riskiest

Young concrete is more porous and hasn't reached its full surface strength. Concrete poured late in the season may face freeze-thaw and salt before it's had much time to cure. That's when surface **scaling** is most likely.

**Protect it by:**

- **Not using rock salt or deicers** on new concrete during its first winter. Use sand for traction.
- **Rinsing off salt** that cars drip onto the driveway from the road
- **Shoveling promptly** so snow doesn't melt and refreeze on the surface
- **Sealing** once the concrete has cured, if timing allows before winter

## Other things to avoid in the first weeks

- **Heavy point loads**, like jack stands, dumpster wheels or ladders on the edges
- **Driving on the edges**, where the slab is weakest
- **Pressure washing** in the first weeks
- **Leaving objects or mats** on the surface while it cures, which can discolor it

## What to expect as it cures

- **Color variation** evens out over the first weeks and months
- **Hairline cracks at control joints** are the joints doing their job. They're designed to be where the concrete cracks.
- **Minor surface crazing** (fine surface lines) is common and usually cosmetic
