On many Southeastern Michigan homes, the first sign of porch trouble isn't the slab. It's the top of the porch walls. Limestone or concrete caps loosen and shift, brick on the knee walls starts to crack or fall out, and railings work loose. Caught early, this is a [porch restoration](/porch-restorations), not a rebuild.

## Why porch caps fail

Porch caps, the flat stones or concrete pieces along the top of the porch walls, are the roof of those walls. Their joints are the weak point:

1. **Joint mortar wears out.** The joints between caps crack and open.
2. **Water gets underneath.** Rain and snowmelt soak the mortar bed and the brick below.
3. **Freeze-thaw lifts the caps.** Ice under the cap pushes it up and breaks its bond.
4. **The wall below deteriorates.** Once water has a path in, the top courses of brick loosen and spall.

Salt tracked onto the porch and snow piled on top of the walls both make it worse.

## Warning signs

- Caps that **rock, slide or have visibly lifted**
- **Open joints** between caps, or weeds growing out of them
- **Loose, cracked or spalling brick** on the top courses of the walls
- **Railings that wobble** at their base
- **White staining** ([efflorescence](/blog/efflorescence-white-stains-on-brick)) running down the wall below the caps

## How a porch cap and wall restoration works

1. **Check whether the porch has moved.** If the porch has settled, cracked through or pulled away from the house, putting new caps on a moving base wastes money. That's a [rebuild](/porch-rebuilds).
2. **Remove the caps** and any loose brick below them.
3. **Rebuild the top courses** of brick where needed, using matched brick and compatible mortar.
4. **Reset the caps** on a full mortar bed. Replace cracked or broken caps with matching stone or concrete.
5. **Seal the cap joints** so water stays out.
6. **Re-anchor railings** in sound masonry.
7. **Repoint** the rest of the wall where the joints are worn. See [tuckpointing](/tuckpointing).

## Restoration or rebuild?

Restoration makes sense when the porch is level, tight to the house, and the damage is limited to caps, the top courses of brick, and surfaces. If the slab is cracked through and sloping, or the walls are bulging, read [what happens during a porch rebuild](/blog/porch-rebuild-process). Our guide to [why brick porches and steps fail](/blog/porch-and-step-problems) covers the bigger picture.

The sooner caps are reset, the less brick has to be rebuilt underneath them. A spring or fall check of your porch caps is one of the cheapest bits of masonry maintenance there is.
