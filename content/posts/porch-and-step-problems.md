Drive through almost any post-war neighborhood in Southeastern Michigan and you'll see the same thing: brick front porches with cracked caps, steps that tilt away from the house, and slabs with a gap where they meet the foundation. These problems have common causes, and knowing them helps you pick the right fix.

## The usual causes

**Shallow footings.** Many older porches were built without footings that reach below the frost line — typically 42 inches in Southeastern Michigan. Every winter the ground freezes, expands and lifts the porch a little, then lets it back down unevenly.

**Settled fill.** Porches are often built over backfill next to the foundation. That soil keeps compacting for years, and the porch slowly drops or tilts.

**Water at the top.** Caps and slab surfaces that let water in are just as destructive. Water gets under a limestone or concrete cap, freezes, and lifts it. Water in the brick side walls spalls the brick.

**Salt.** Rock salt accelerates scaling on concrete slabs and steps, especially ones that were never sealed.

## When restoration makes sense

If the porch hasn't moved — it's level, tight to the house, and the walls are straight — the problems are on the surface, and [porch restoration](/porch-restorations) is usually the right call. That can mean resetting or replacing caps, replacing and repointing brick, resurfacing the slab, and repairing step edges.

## When it needs a rebuild

If the porch has settled, pulled away from the house, cracked through its slab, or has walls that bulge, the problem is underneath. Putting new brick and caps on a porch that's still moving just means doing it again in a few years. A [porch rebuild](/porch-rebuilds) starts over with footings below frost depth, a solid foundation, and a new slab and steps.

## Steps deserve special attention

Steps separate from porches more often than any other element because they're frequently built on their own, shallower base. They're also where people get hurt. Uneven risers — even a small difference from one step to the next — cause trips. When steps are rebuilt, the rise and run of every step should be consistent. ## A simple self-check

- Lay a level on the porch slab. It should slope slightly **away** from the house.
- Look where the porch meets the house. A growing gap means it's moving.
- Check the caps. Rocking or visibly lifted caps mean water is getting underneath.
- Walk the steps. Do any rock, tilt, or feel taller than the others?
