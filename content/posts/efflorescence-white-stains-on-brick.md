A white, chalky film on brick is one of the most common questions we get from Southeastern Michigan homeowners. It shows up on chimneys, basement walls, porches and brand-new brickwork alike. It has a name, **efflorescence**, and it's usually a message about water, not a problem with the brick itself.

## What efflorescence is

Brick, mortar and concrete all contain small amounts of water-soluble salts. When water soaks into the masonry, it dissolves those salts and carries them toward the surface. When the water evaporates, the salts are left behind as a white or grayish powder or crust.

So efflorescence needs three things: salts, water, and a path for that water to travel to the surface and evaporate. Take away the water and the efflorescence stops coming back.

## When it's harmless and when it isn't

**Usually harmless:**

- On new brickwork or a new chimney during its first year, as construction moisture dries out
- A light, even haze after a wet spring that fades in dry weather
- Small amounts near grade on a wall that otherwise looks sound

**Worth investigating:**

- Heavy white crust that returns every time it's cleaned
- Efflorescence concentrated under a cap, sill, crown or downspout
- White deposits on basement or foundation walls along with dampness inside
- Efflorescence together with spalling brick or crumbling mortar

Those patterns point to a steady water source. The same water that brings salts to the surface can also freeze in the brick and cause [spalling](/blog/brick-spalling-causes-and-fixes).

## Where the water usually comes from

- **Worn mortar joints** that let rain into the wall. [Tuckpointing](/tuckpointing) closes them.
- **Cracked chimney crowns and missing caps** that let water into the top of the chimney
- **Porch caps and wall copings** with open joints
- **Gutters and downspouts** that overflow or discharge against the wall
- **Grading and landscaping** that keep soil and mulch piled against the brick
- **Ground moisture** wicking into foundation and basement walls

## How to clean efflorescence safely

1. **Wait for dry weather.** Cleaning a wet wall just redissolves the salts and pulls them back in.
2. **Dry brush first.** A stiff nylon brush removes most light efflorescence. Avoid wire brushes, which can leave rust marks and scratch the brick face.
3. **Rinse lightly if needed.** Use low pressure, and let the wall dry fully afterward.
4. **Be careful with acids.** Masonry cleaners can remove stubborn deposits, but the wrong product or concentration can etch brick and damage mortar. On older or softer brick, it's worth having a mason do this.

Cleaning treats the symptom. If it keeps coming back, the fix is stopping the water.

## What a mason will check

When we look at recurring efflorescence, we trace it back: the condition of the joints, the caps and crowns above the stained area, how the gutters and downspouts drain, and how the ground slopes at the base of the wall. Often the repair is modest, such as repointing a section, resetting a cap or redirecting a downspout, and it prevents much bigger [brick repairs](/brick-repair) later.
