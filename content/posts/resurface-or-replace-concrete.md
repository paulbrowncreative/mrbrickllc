Concrete that looks worn out isn't always worn out. Scaling, pitting and stains often only affect the top layer of an otherwise solid slab. When that's the case, [resurfacing](/concrete-resurfacing) can give you a new surface for much less than tearing out and replacing. When the slab itself has failed, resurfacing is money wasted. Here's how to tell the difference.

## What resurfacing is

Resurfacing applies a thin, bonded overlay over existing concrete after the surface has been cleaned and prepared, and any surface damage repaired. It can be finished smooth, broom-textured or decorative, and it's sealed afterward.

It's a new **skin**, not new structure. It depends entirely on the slab underneath being sound.

## Good candidates for resurfacing

- **Scaling or flaking** surfaces, often from salt, on a slab that's still level
- **Pitting and exposed aggregate** from wear
- **Stains and discoloration**
- **Shallow, stable surface cracks**
- Porches, walks, patios and driveways that are **level, drain properly and don't rock or move**

## Signs it needs replacing instead

- **Cracks that go through the slab**, especially with one side higher than the other
- **Sunken, heaved or tilted sections.** Sometimes these can be [leveled](/blog/sunken-concrete-slab-leveling) first.
- **Crumbling edges** and broken-up areas
- **Water pooling** because the slope is wrong
- A slab that **sounds hollow** when tapped, meaning voids underneath
- **Widespread cracking** across most of the surface

## The checks a contractor should make

Before quoting a resurface, a contractor should walk the slab, look at crack patterns and edges, check whether sections have moved, look at drainage, and tap for hollow areas. If you're offered a resurface without anyone checking whether the slab is moving, ask why.

## Partial replacement: the middle option

On driveways and walks, a slab is usually divided into panels by control joints. If only a few panels have failed, they can be cut out and replaced while the rest are repaired or resurfaced. The trade-off is color: new concrete won't exactly match old concrete. Read [driveway cracks: repair or replace](/blog/driveway-cracks-repair-or-replace).

## Making a resurface last

- Proper surface preparation, the step that decides whether the overlay bonds
- Repairing the causes of damage, such as downspouts, poor drainage and heavy salt use
- Sealing after the overlay cures, and resealing on schedule. See [sealing concrete in Michigan](/blog/sealing-concrete-driveway-michigan).
- Sand instead of heavy salt in winter
