Masonry is built to last decades, and so are its mistakes. A porch on shallow footings, a chimney without a proper crown, or brick repointed with the wrong mortar can look fine on day one and fail within a few winters. These ten questions help you compare contractors on what actually matters.

## 1. Are you licensed and insured?

Ask to see proof of both. Insurance protects you if someone is hurt or property is damaged during the job. It matters most on roofs and chimneys.

## 2. What exactly is included in the quote?

A good quote spells out the scope: demolition and haul-away, excavation, base or footings, materials, finishes, clean-up, and anything that's excluded. Vague quotes make it impossible to compare contractors.

## 3. How deep will the footings or base be?

For porches, steps, walls and fireplaces, footings should go below the frost line. For patios, walks and driveways, ask about base depth and compaction. Read [why frost depth matters](/blog/frost-depth-footings-michigan).

## 4. How will you deal with water?

Most masonry failures are water problems. Ask how the work will shed water: crown overhangs, sloped slabs, drainage behind retaining walls, cap joints and downspouts.

## 5. What mortar will you use, and will it match?

On repointing and brick repair, the mortar should match the existing joints in color and be compatible in strength with the brick. On older homes, that usually means a softer mix. Read [how often brick needs tuckpointing](/blog/how-often-does-brick-need-tuckpointing).

## 6. Can you match my brick or stone?

Ask to see samples before work starts. On older homes an exact match may no longer be made, and a good contractor will tell you so up front.

## 7. Is a permit required, and who pulls it?

Porch rebuilds, steps, driveway aprons and some walls often need permits in Southeastern Michigan communities. Your contractor should know and handle it.

## 8. What's the timeline, and how does weather affect it?

Mortar and concrete need the right temperatures to cure. Ask for a realistic schedule, and how you'll be kept updated if weather pushes it back.

## 9. What warranty do you offer?

Get it in writing and know what it covers. Mr. Brick provides a 10-year warranty on all new construction.

## 10. Can I see your work and read your reviews?

Look at project photos of work like yours, and read reviews from real customers. See our [project gallery](/gallery) and [customer reviews](/reviews).

## Red flags

- Pressure to sign on the spot, or large cash deposits before any work
- Recommending a full rebuild without explaining why a repair won't work
- Vague answers about footings, base or drainage
- No proof of insurance
- A price far below everyone else with no explanation of what's different
