A patio is one of the best ways to add usable living space to a Michigan home. The difference between one you use every evening and one that sits empty usually comes down to planning: the right size, the right spot, and a base that keeps it flat through our winters.

## Start with how you'll use it

Before choosing materials, decide what happens on the patio:

- **Dining.** A table and chairs need room to pull chairs out and walk around them.
- **Lounging.** Sofas, sectionals and lounge chairs take more space than people expect.
- **Cooking.** A grill or [outdoor kitchen](/blog/outdoor-kitchen-planning-guide) needs clearance and a safe distance from the house.
- **Fire.** A fire pit or [outdoor fireplace](/fireplace-services) needs seating around it and clearance from structures.

A common mistake is building a patio that fits the furniture exactly, with no room to move around it. Lay out your furniture on the lawn, or mark the shape with a hose, before you commit to a size.

## Location, sun and flow

- **Access** from the kitchen or back door makes a patio get used
- **Sun and shade** at the times you'll use it
- **Privacy** from neighbors and the street
- **Level ground**, since steep yards may need steps or [retaining walls](/retaining-wall-services)
- **Utilities and drainage** paths underground

## Choosing a material

- **Pavers.** Flexible, repairable and available in many styles. See [stamped concrete vs. pavers](/blog/stamped-concrete-vs-pavers).
- **Natural stone.** Flagstone and cut stone for a premium, timeless look.
- **Poured or stamped concrete.** Economical, with decorative finishes available.

## The part you don't see: base and drainage

- **Excavation** deep enough for a proper base, with soft spots removed
- **Compacted aggregate base**, installed and compacted in layers
- **Slope away from the house**, so rain never runs toward the foundation
- **Edge restraint** for pavers, and control joints for concrete

This is what keeps a patio flat. Read [why pavers sink and shift](/blog/sunken-pavers-causes-and-fixes).

## Add-ons worth planning from the start

- **Seat walls** that double as extra seating and define the space
- **Steps** from a door or deck, built with even rises. See [choosing front steps](/blog/brick-vs-stone-vs-concrete-steps).
- **Fire pits and fireplaces**
- **Outdoor kitchens and grill islands**
- **Lighting conduit** run before the base goes in

Planning these up front costs far less than cutting into a finished patio later.
