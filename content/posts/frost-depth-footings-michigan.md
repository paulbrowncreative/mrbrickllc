Ask a Michigan mason why a porch pulled away from a house or why a set of steps tipped, and you'll often hear the same answer: the footings weren't deep enough. Frost depth is one of the least visible parts of a masonry project and one of the most important.

## What frost heave is

When water in the soil freezes, it expands, and it can lift anything resting on or in that frozen layer. As the ground thaws in spring, it softens and settles. Repeat that every winter and a porch, step or wall built on shallow soil gets pushed up, dropped, and tilted a little more each year.

The **frost line** is roughly how deep the ground freezes in a typical winter. Build the bottom of a footing below it, on soil that doesn't freeze, and the structure isn't lifted.

## How deep is the frost line in Southeastern Michigan?

Footings in Southeastern Michigan commonly need to be **42 inches deep**. Your city's building department sets the exact requirement and may inspect footings before they're poured. Always confirm locally.

## What needs frost-depth footings

- **Porches**, and the steps attached to them. Read [the porch rebuild process](/blog/porch-rebuild-process).
- **Standalone masonry steps**
- **Masonry walls, pillars and planters**
- **Additions, garages and foundations**
- **Outdoor fireplaces and masonry outdoor kitchens**. See [outdoor kitchen planning](/blog/outdoor-kitchen-planning-guide).
- **Many retaining walls**, depending on type and height. See [why retaining walls lean](/blog/why-retaining-walls-lean).

**Flatwork**, meaning patios, walks and driveways, is usually built differently: on a thick, compacted aggregate base that drains well, which limits frost movement without full-depth footings.

## Why so many older porches moved

Many porches and steps built decades ago in Southeastern Michigan sit on shallow footings or on fill that was never compacted. Over the years, frost heave and settlement pulled them away from the house or tipped them toward the yard. No amount of new brick or resurfacing fixes a porch that's still moving. It has to be rebuilt on proper footings. Read [why brick porches and steps fail](/blog/porch-and-step-problems).

## Drainage matters too

Frost heave needs water. Keeping water away from footings with downspouts, grading and drainage behind walls reduces frost movement and settlement, even on well-built foundations.

## Questions to ask before a porch, step or wall project

- How deep will the footings be?
- Will they be inspected before the pour?
- How will water be kept away from them?
