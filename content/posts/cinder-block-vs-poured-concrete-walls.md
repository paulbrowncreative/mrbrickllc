When a project needs a structural wall, whether it's a garage, a foundation for an addition, the base of a new porch or a retaining wall, the two common choices are concrete block and poured concrete. Both can be excellent. The right pick depends on the job, the site and how the wall will be finished.

## First, a note on names

Most people say "cinder block," but true cinder block, made with coal cinders, has largely disappeared. What's laid today are **concrete masonry units (CMU)**: hollow concrete blocks laid in mortar, often with steel reinforcement and grout in the cores. We use "block" for both below.

## How block walls are built

Block walls go up one course at a time on a concrete footing. Where the wall carries load or holds back soil, vertical steel is set in the cores and filled with grout, and horizontal reinforcement or bond beams tie the courses together.

**Where block shines:**

- **Access and site conditions.** Block can be carried and placed where a concrete truck and forms can't easily go, such as tight side yards, backyards and porches against a house.
- **Porch and step foundations.** Block is the standard base for many brick and concrete porches.
- **Brick and stone veneer.** Block makes a natural backup wall for a veneer finish.
- **Repairs.** Individual damaged blocks and deteriorated mortar can be repaired or replaced.

## How poured concrete walls are built

Poured walls need forms set on a footing, reinforcement placed, and concrete delivered and placed in one continuous operation.

**Where poured concrete shines:**

- **Continuous walls with fewer joints**, which can mean fewer paths for water
- **Large, simple runs** where forming is efficient
- **Specific engineered designs** that call for cast-in-place concrete

## What drives the cost of each

We don't publish prices because every site is different, but these are the factors that move the number on either wall type:

- Wall height and length
- Excavation, and how much soil has to come out
- Access for equipment, concrete trucks and pumps
- Reinforcement required by the design or the building department
- Drainage and waterproofing details
- The finish: bare block, parge coat, or brick or stone veneer

## Michigan-specific details that matter more than the material

- **Footings below the frost line.** Frost heave doesn't care what the wall is made of. Read [why frost depth matters](/blog/frost-depth-footings-michigan).
- **Drainage behind any wall that holds back soil.** Water pressure is the number one reason [retaining walls lean](/blog/why-retaining-walls-lean).
- **Reinforcement sized to the wall.** A tall wall or one carrying load needs it, whichever material you choose.

## Which should you choose?

For porch bases, garage walls, small foundations, block backup for brick, and walls with tight access, block is often the practical choice. For long, simple foundation runs where forms and trucks can get in easily, poured walls make sense.
