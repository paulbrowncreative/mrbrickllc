Two concrete driveways can look identical on the day they're poured and perform completely differently ten winters later. The difference is almost entirely in the parts you can't see once the job is done. Here's what goes into a [concrete driveway](/concrete-driveway-installation) built for Southeastern Michigan.

## Signs it's time to replace, not repair

- **Widespread cracking** across many panels, not just one or two
- **Heaved or sunken sections** that trip people or scrape cars
- **Water pooling** because the slope has changed
- **Crumbling edges and apron**
- **Surface scaling** on most of the driveway, not just a patch

If the damage is limited, read [driveway cracks: repair or replace](/blog/driveway-cracks-repair-or-replace) first.

## 1. Tear-out and haul-away

The old concrete is broken up and removed. This is when soft spots, old tree roots and poor fill show up, and they need to be dealt with before anything new goes down.

## 2. Excavation and base

A driveway is only as good as what it sits on. Soft or organic soil is removed, and a layer of **compacted aggregate base** is installed. That base spreads the load of vehicles, drains water from under the slab and reduces frost movement. A thin or uncompacted base is the most common reason driveways crack and settle early.

## 3. Forms and slope

Forms set the finished shape and height. The driveway needs a steady slope so water runs **away from the garage and house** and toward the street or yard, never into the garage or against the foundation.

## 4. Reinforcement

Reinforcement helps hold cracked concrete together and control how it moves. The right approach depends on the slab, the soil and the loads it will carry.

## 5. Placement, finishing and control joints

- **Finish.** A broom finish gives traction in snow and rain, and it's the standard for Michigan driveways. Decorative stamped borders or a full [stamped](/stamped-concrete) surface are options.
- **Control joints.** Concrete will crack. Joints cut at the right spacing and depth make it crack in straight lines at the joint instead of randomly across the slab.

## 6. Curing and first-winter care

New concrete needs time before cars drive on it, and protection from salt in its first winter. Read [new concrete care](/blog/new-concrete-curing-and-first-winter). Sealing after it cures adds protection. See [sealing concrete in Michigan](/blog/sealing-concrete-driveway-michigan).

## What drives driveway cost

- Size and thickness
- Tear-out of the old driveway, and how much fill or base is needed
- The apron and any street or sidewalk work your city requires
- Access for trucks and equipment
- Finish: broom, colored or stamped borders, or a full stamped surface
- Permits, where required

## Questions to ask your contractor

- What base will go under the slab, and how deep?
- How will it slope, and where will the water go?
- Where will the control joints be?
- When can I drive on it, and how should I treat it the first winter?
- Is a permit required in my city?
