A stain on the ceiling next to the chimney, a damp wall in the attic, or water in the firebox all say the same thing: water is getting in around the chimney. What they don't tell you is where. Chimney leaks get blamed on the roof all the time, but the masonry is just as often the culprit.

## The four places chimney water comes from

### 1. The crown and the top of the chimney

A cracked or poorly built crown lets water into the top of the masonry, where it runs down inside the chimney. It often shows up far below where it got in. Read [chimney crown vs. chimney cap](/blog/chimney-crown-vs-chimney-cap) for how the top of a chimney should shed water.

### 2. The flue opening

Without a cap, rain falls straight down the flue. Look for water, rust or staining in the firebox or around the damper.

### 3. The brick and mortar

Brick absorbs water, and open mortar joints let in even more, especially in wind-driven rain. On the inside, this often shows as dampness in the attic along the chimney or stains that grow during long storms.

[[photo:chimney-terracotta-flues|The top of a chimney during repair, with the clay flue tiles exposed.]]

### 4. The flashing

Flashing is the metal that seals the joint between the chimney and the roof. Failed or poorly installed flashing leaks where the chimney meets the roof, usually right at the ceiling line or in the attic next to the chimney. Flashing is part of the roofing system.

## Clues that help narrow it down

- **Only during wind-driven storms** points to brick and mortar or flashing on the windward side
- **Water in the firebox** points to the flue opening or the crown
- **Stains right where the chimney meets the ceiling** point to flashing, or water running down inside the chimney
- **White staining or flaking brick outside** ([efflorescence](/blog/efflorescence-white-stains-on-brick) or [spalling](/blog/brick-spalling-causes-and-fixes)) means the masonry is absorbing water
- **Stains that appear days after rain** means water is travelling through saturated masonry slowly

## Why leaks often have more than one source

Chimneys that leak have usually been exposed for a while, so it's common to find a cracked crown *and* open joints, or failed flashing *and* spalled top courses. Fixing only one source can reduce the leak without stopping it.

## What a masonry inspection should cover

When we check a leaking chimney, we look at the crown, the cap, every face of the brick and mortar (especially the top few feet), the flue tiles, and the visible condition of the flashing. If the flashing looks like the problem, we'll tell you, and you'll know what to ask your roofer to look at.

[Chimney repair](/chimney-repair) that stops a leak usually means a new or repaired crown, a cap, and [tuckpointing](/tuckpointing) of the exposed brick, before water damages the brick enough to need a [rebuild](/chimney-rebuilding).
