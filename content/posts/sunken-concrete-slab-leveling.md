A sidewalk panel that's dropped an inch, a patio that tips toward the house, or a driveway slab that collects a puddle: sunken concrete is more than an eyesore. It's a trip hazard, and when it slopes the wrong way it sends water straight at your foundation. The good news is that a sunken slab that's still in one piece can often be lifted back into place instead of replaced.

## Why concrete sinks

Concrete rarely sinks on its own. The soil under it moves:

- **Washout.** Downspouts, sump discharge or poor grading send water under the slab and carry soil away.
- **Uncompacted fill.** Soil backfilled against a foundation or over a trench was never compacted, and it settles for years.
- **Erosion at the edges** along lawns and beds
- **Frost and moisture changes** in the soil through the seasons

## Why it's worth fixing

- **Trip hazards.** A lip of even a small height on a walk or step is exactly where people fall.
- **Water toward the house.** Patios, walks and stoops that slope toward the foundation push water where you least want it. See [foundation cracks: when to worry](/blog/foundation-cracks-when-to-worry).
- **More damage over time.** Water collecting on or under a sunken slab speeds up cracking and freeze-thaw damage.

## When leveling is the right fix

[Concrete leveling](/concrete-leveling) lifts settled sections back into place and fills the voids underneath. It's a good fit when:

- The slab is **mostly intact**, not broken into many pieces
- The concrete surface is in **reasonable condition**
- The goal is to remove a **trip hazard** or restore **drainage slope**

After lifting, joints and cracks are sealed to keep water from washing out the soil again.

## When replacement makes more sense

- The slab is **broken into multiple pieces** or crumbling
- The surface is badly **scaled or deteriorated**. Resurfacing may be an option if it's lifted. Read [resurface or replace](/blog/resurface-or-replace-concrete).
- The slab needs to be a **different size, shape or height**
- The base under it needs to be **rebuilt and compacted**

## Fix the cause

Whichever route you take, deal with the water that caused the settlement:

- Extend or redirect **downspouts** away from walks and patios
- **Regrade** soil so it slopes away from the house
- **Seal joints** between slabs and along the foundation
- Keep **sump discharge** away from concrete
