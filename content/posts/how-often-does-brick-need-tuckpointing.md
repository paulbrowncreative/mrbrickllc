There's no single number of years after which a brick house needs tuckpointing. Mortar wears at very different rates depending on where it is on the building and how much water it sees. What you can do is learn which areas wear first, check them every year or two, and repoint before open joints start damaging the brick.

## What tuckpointing does and why mortar wears out

Mortar is designed to be the sacrificial part of a brick wall. It's softer than the brick, so it's the part that erodes, cracks and gets replaced, rather than the brick itself. [Tuckpointing, or repointing,](/tuckpointing) means grinding out deteriorated mortar to a consistent depth and packing in new mortar, tooled to match the original joint.

Rain, wind, and especially Michigan's freeze-thaw cycles slowly wear joints down. Once they're recessed, water sits in them longer and the wear accelerates.

## Which parts of a brick house wear out first

The same house can have joints that are fine on one wall and failing on another. The usual order, from fastest to slowest:

1. **Chimneys**, especially the top few feet above the roofline. They're exposed on all four sides. See [chimney repair](/chimney-repair).
2. **Parapets, pillars, planters and porch walls.** Freestanding masonry gets wet from both sides and the top.
3. **Walls under downspouts, valleys and leaky gutters**, where water concentrates.
4. **South and west walls**, which get the most sun and weather swings.
5. **Brick near grade**, where splash-back and ground moisture keep it wet.
6. **Sheltered walls under deep eaves**, which often last longest.

That's why spot repointing is so common: the chimney and one porch wall may need it long before the rest of the house.

## A two-minute check you can do yourself

Walk around the house on a dry day and look closely at the joints:

- **Recessed joints.** If the mortar sits a quarter inch or more behind the brick face, it's time.
- **The key test.** Press a key or flat screwdriver into a joint. If it scrapes out easily and leaves a groove, the mortar has lost its strength.
- **Cracks along the joints**, especially stair-step patterns
- **Sandy mortar** at the base of the wall or on the ground below
- **Loose or rocking bricks**
- **White staining** ([efflorescence](/blog/efflorescence-white-stains-on-brick)) or damp spots inside near exterior walls

Use binoculars for the chimney rather than climbing onto the roof.

## Why repointing early saves money

Repointing is the least invasive brick repair there is. Wait too long and water gets into the brick itself, which leads to spalling, loose units and eventually section rebuilds. That's the difference between a tuckpointing bill and a [brick repair](/brick-repair) or partial rebuild bill. Our guide to [tuckpointing vs. rebuilding](/blog/tuckpointing-vs-rebuilding) walks through how to tell which one a wall needs.

## What good tuckpointing looks like

- Old mortar removed to a consistent depth, not just smeared over
- Mortar matched to the wall in color and compatible in strength. On older, softer brick that usually means a softer mix.
- Joints packed in layers and tooled to the same profile as the original
- Clean brick faces when the job is done
- Any cracked or loose bricks found along the way replaced

Done this way, new joints read as part of the original wall rather than a patch.
