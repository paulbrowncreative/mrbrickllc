Finding a crack in your foundation is unsettling, but many foundation cracks are normal, and many problem cracks are very fixable. The pattern of the crack tells you a lot about what's causing it and who you should call.

**This guide is general information, not a structural assessment.** When in doubt, have the wall looked at in person.

## Cracks that are often normal

**Hairline cracks** in poured concrete walls, especially vertical ones, are common as concrete shrinks while it cures. If they're thin, stable and dry, they're usually cosmetic.

**Small vertical cracks** that aren't growing and aren't letting in water are typically not a structural concern, though sealing them keeps water out.

## Cracks worth a closer look

**Stair-step cracks in block walls.** Cracks following the mortar joints in a stair-step pattern can mean settlement or pressure on the wall. Small, stable ones may just need repointing. Wide, growing ones need evaluation.

**Cracks that let water in.** Even a structurally harmless crack is a problem if it leaks. Repairs should address the crack and where the water is coming from.

**Cracks that are widening.** Mark the ends and the width with a pencil and date. If the crack grows over months, the wall is still moving.

## Cracks that need prompt evaluation

**Horizontal cracks**, especially in block walls, often mean soil and water pressure pushing on the wall.

**Bowing or leaning walls** mean the wall is being pushed inward.

**Cracks with displacement**, where one side has shifted past the other.

For these, an evaluation by a structural engineer is often the right first step. We'll tell you when we think that's the case, and we'll carry out repairs to the engineer's specification.

## The water connection

Most foundation problems are water problems. Water in the soil adds pressure against walls, softens the ground under footings, and freezes against the foundation in winter. The most effective foundation work often starts outside:

- **Downspouts** extended well away from the house
- **Grading** that slopes soil away from the foundation
- **Gutters** kept clear
- **Patios, walks and stoops** that slope away from the house, not toward it. See [sunken concrete slabs](/blog/sunken-concrete-slab-leveling).

## What foundation repair can include

- Assessment of cracks and wall condition
- Crack repair and sealing
- Block replacement and repointing on block walls
- Drainage corrections at the foundation
- Referral to a structural engineer when warranted
