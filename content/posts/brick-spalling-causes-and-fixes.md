If the face of your brick is flaking, popping off in chips, or crumbling to powder, you're looking at **spalling**. It's one of the most common brick problems on Southeastern Michigan homes, and one of the most misunderstood. The good news is that it's almost always fixable. The bad news is that the fix isn't a coat of paint, sealer or patching compound.

## What brick spalling actually is

Brick is porous. It absorbs water, and in a climate like ours that water freezes and thaws many times every winter. Each freeze expands the water trapped just under the surface of the brick. Eventually the outer face can't take the pressure and lets go, leaving a rough, pitted brick behind.

Once the hard outer face is gone, the softer core underneath absorbs water even faster, so spalling speeds up. A wall that loses a few brick faces this winter can lose a lot more over the next few.

## The five usual causes on Michigan homes

Spalling is a water problem first and a brick problem second. When we see it, we look for where the water is coming from:

1. **Open mortar joints.** Worn, recessed or cracked joints let water straight into the wall. This is the most common cause, and [tuckpointing](/tuckpointing) is the fix.
2. **Failed caps, sills and crowns.** Water that gets in from the top of a chimney, porch wall, pillar or planter soaks down through every course of brick below it.
3. **Downspouts and splash-back.** Brick near grade, near a downspout outlet or under a leaking gutter stays wet much longer than the rest of the wall.
4. **The wrong sealer.** Film-forming sealers and some paints keep liquid water out but trap vapor inside. The trapped moisture freezes behind the coating and pops the face off.
5. **Mortar that's harder than the brick.** Repointing soft, older brick with modern high-strength mortar forces the brick to take the stress instead of the joint. The brick edges crack and spall first.

## Why patching and sealing don't last

Masonry patch products and "brick paint" are tempting because they're cheap and quick. On spalled brick they tend to fail for the same reason the brick did. They hold moisture against a wall that needs to dry out, and they can't move with the wall through freeze-thaw. Most pop off within a few seasons, and the brick underneath is usually worse.

Sealers have their place on some masonry, but they're not a cure for spalling. If water is getting in through joints or from the top, sealing the face only changes where it gets trapped.

## How a mason repairs spalled brick

A lasting repair has two parts: stop the water, then replace what's damaged.

- **Find and fix the source.** Repoint open joints, rebuild or replace failed caps and crowns, redirect downspouts, and correct grading that sends water toward the wall.
- **Cut out the damaged units.** Spalled bricks are removed without disturbing the sound brick around them.
- **Set matched brick.** Replacement brick should be as close as possible in size, color and texture. On older homes an exact match isn't always still made, so you should see samples first.
- **Use compatible mortar.** The new mortar should match the existing joints in color and be softer than the brick, so the joint, not the brick, takes future movement.

Where spalling has spread across a section and the bricks have lost their bond with each other, [brick repair](/brick-repair) may mean taking that section down and relaying it rather than replacing bricks one at a time.

## When to call someone

A handful of flaked faces on a sheltered wall can wait for a planned repair. Don't wait when:

- Spalling is on a chimney, parapet or porch column where pieces can fall
- Bricks are loose, not just flaked
- The wall bulges, leans or has cracks running through brick, not just mortar
- You're seeing water stains inside near the damaged area
